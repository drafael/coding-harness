# Context guide

Load this guide when genre conventions affect an edit. The core preservation rules always apply.

## Technical documentation

Prefer observable behavior, exact interfaces, prerequisites, failure modes, and runnable instructions. Keep code, commands, identifiers, paths, protocol terms, and established concepts unchanged. Terms such as API surface, primitive, vector, passive, and eventually consistent may be precise rather than jargon.

Do not turn every paragraph into short imperative sentences. Explanations still need relationships and context. Preserve normative terms such as MUST, SHOULD, and MAY when they follow a specification.

## Academic and scientific prose

Preserve citations, qualifications, terminology, and the distinction between observation, inference, and hypothesis. Passive voice may be appropriate when the method or result matters more than the actor. Do not replace cautious language with certainty or insert a stronger causal claim.

Avoid invented sources and do not generalize beyond the evidence provided.

## Legal and regulated text

Treat contractual clauses, disclosures, policy language, and regulatory terminology as protected unless the user explicitly authorizes substantive editing. Plain-language improvements must not alter obligations, permissions, exceptions, defined terms, scope, or legal effect.

If an edit might change interpretation, explain the concern instead of silently rewriting it.

## Marketing and product copy

Promotional language is not automatically wrong in marketing. Remove unsupported superlatives and generic excitement, but preserve the intended persuasive function. Prefer specific capabilities, evidence, and customer-relevant outcomes over empty claims.

Do not create testimonials, adoption figures, comparisons, or guarantees.

## Casual and personal writing

Preserve contractions, fragments, humor, slang, and idiosyncratic punctuation when they belong to the author. Do not polish the text into corporate prose. First person is appropriate only when present in the source or explicitly requested.

## Correspondence

Courtesy is not the same as chatbot filler. Keep greetings, thanks, and invitations that serve the relationship. Remove canned praise and repeated offers that do not fit the exchange.

## Exact and quoted material

Do not rewrite direct quotations, titles, citations, transcripts, code samples, commands, identifiers, URLs, structured data, or text marked verbatim. Edit the surrounding prose instead. If quoted material appears inaccurate, flag it separately rather than changing the quote.

## Unclear genre

Infer conservatively from the text. Preserve the current level of formality and personality. If a major voice change would be required, ask the user rather than guessing.
